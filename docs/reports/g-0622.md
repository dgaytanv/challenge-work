Short interim report (the 60-minute rule). One new hard result, and an honest status on the synthesis clock.

## 1. New result: N=400 now compiles and is bit-exact
Campaign 1's §8 records that the io_parallel design at N=400 **never compiled** — g++ time-boxed and killed
at 2,710 s (45.2 min) without finishing — so the bit-exactness claim only ever held at N=16 and
"buildability at 400 tokens is unproven". With `--pf 1` (folded token loop) and patch **P3** (quantizer mask
emitted once and looped instead of one line of C++ per element):

| | campaign 1 | campaign 2 |
|---|---|---|
| g++ compile of the N=400 io_parallel design | **killed at 2,710 s, unfinished** | **183 s, finished** |
| C-simulation vs Keras at N=400, 16 events | never obtained | **max&#124;d&#124; = 0.000e+00, 100.00 % of elements exact** |

Source: `quant/g6_g6b_N400_pf1_foldq_csim.json`. Design: G6b, N=400, 6-bit cap + ReLU
(`gF-bits6-relu-l1t` weights), `xcvu13p-flga2577-2-e`, 5 ns (200 MHz), io_parallel, pf=1, einsum ReuseFactor 400.

So the chain float → HGQ2 Keras → torch emulator → hls4ml C++ is now exact **at the token count the eval set
actually uses**, not only at the N=16 stand-in. That closes campaign-1 open item 2's numerical half; the
hardware half is what the synthesis below is for.

## 3. Two judgement calls since the last report
* **Swapped the N=16 anchor to the folded design.** I had the *unpatched* N=16 running as the sanity anchor;
  at 2.5 M instructions it was twice the size of the folded N=400 and would have anchored a design we are not
  shipping. Stopped at 05:24 (partial log `synth/N16_unpatched_csynth_partial.log`), restarted as the folded
  build so the anchor and the deliverable are the same design at two token counts.
* **Reclaimed two CPU cores.** Both stopped runs had left orphaned `clang` children at 95 % CPU — 60 and 41
  minutes of wall clock each, invisible in the Vitis logs. Killing the parent `vitis_hls` does not take them
  with it. Worth knowing for anyone else running these on the shared box.
