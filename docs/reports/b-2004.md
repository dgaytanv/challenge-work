## Design
- **Exact symmetries first** (train only): global phi rotation + eta reflection with prob 0.5, applied
  to valid rows only so zero rows stay exactly zero.
- **Dead regions**: with prob `p_clean`=0.15 none; else `s ~ U(0, s_max=0.85)` and one of five families
  (rect / wedge / strip / cells / towers), chosen uniformly.
- **Milder modes**: charged-only (0.05), neutral-only (0.05), pt scaled by U(0.3,0.9) instead of zeroed (0.10).
- **Curriculum**: `s_max` ramps 0.2 -> 0.85 over the first `warmup_calls`=2000 forwards. `curriculum=False` disables.
- **Eval mode**: coarse cells (0.5), fraction `s` dead, drop prob 0.8, seeded generator that advances
  across batches, so a whole sweep is reproducible from `seed`. Severity 0 returns the input object.

## Decisions I made that you may want to overrule
1. **Severity means "expected fraction of candidates dropped"**, and I rescale every family's shape prior
   to hit it. The literal ranges in wp-b.md (rect d_eta ~ U(0.5,2.5), d_phi ~ U(0.3,1.2), K <= 4) cover at
   most ~19% of the plane, so rectangles could never reach the s=0.8 the tests require. I kept those ranges
   as the *aspect-ratio* prior and scale both dimensions by a common factor to reach s. Same idea for
   wedges/strips (band count from the width prior, width rescaled so M*w*p/span = s).
2. **eta strips span full phi.** wp-b.md gives strips both d_eta ~ U(1.0,2.5) and d_phi ~ U(0.1,0.35),
   which is a small patch rather than a strip, and duplicates the rect family. I made strips full-phi eta
   bands, the natural complement to phi wedges. Say the word and I will add the phi-limited variant.
3. **Eval mode follows wp-b.md literally** (fraction s of cells dead x drop prob 0.8), so its effective
   drop is 0.8*s, not s — visible in the sweep above. Train mode compensates, eval mode does not.
4. Coarse-cell sizes {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} are laid out in one fused flat grid so the family costs one random
   field and one gather rather than three.

## Cross-cutting finding for everyone (not mine to fix)
`PFPreProcessor` normalises pt as `log(pt_i / sum_pt)` where `sum_pt` is the **per-event sum over surviving
candidates**. When a dead region removes candidates, `sum_pt` drops, so *every surviving candidate's* pt
feature shifts by a constant `-log(f)` (f = surviving pt fraction). A dead region therefore moves the whole
event in feature space even for candidates far from the region — a systematic, severity-correlated latent
shift that no amount of attention masking removes. At 50% pt loss that is +0.69 on a batch-normalised
feature. Worth a targeted experiment (normalise by a fixed constant, or by a robust statistic, or feed
`log(sum_pt)` as an explicit token) — I think this is potentially worth more than anything in my own WP.
It sits in `preprocs.py`, which nobody owns. Want me to take it as WP-B2?
