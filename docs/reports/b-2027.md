## 2. WP-B2: why the pt normalisation matters
`PFPreProcessor` encodes pt as `log(pt_i / sum_pt)`, `sum_pt` over **surviving** candidates. Kill a dead
region and `sum_pt` falls, so *every surviving candidate* has its pt feature shifted by the same constant
`-log(f)`, `f` = surviving pt fraction. Candidates nowhere near the dead region move too. Nothing
downstream — masking, readout, architecture — can undo a shift that is already in the input.

### Latent displacement, stock checkpoint, my eval-mode degradation, 512 events
`|dz|` is the distance between clean and degraded latents in units of the clean-latent std.
"frozen" repeats the degraded event but normalises pt by the **clean** `sum_pt`, isolating the denominator.

| severity | pt lost | -log f | in BN units | \|dz\| stock | \|dz\| frozen | removed by freezing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0.155 | 0.169 | 0.458 | 0.4280 | 0.1871 | **56.3%** |
| 0.4 | 0.314 | 0.376 | 1.021 | 0.7966 | 0.3449 | **56.7%** |
| 0.6 | 0.475 | 0.644 | 1.746 | 1.0239 | 0.4784 | **53.3%** |
| 0.8 | 0.637 | 1.014 | 2.751 | 1.2270 | 0.6218 | **49.3%** |

The pt feature's BatchNorm running std is 0.369, so at severity 0.8 the shift is 2.75 batch-norm units.
**Roughly half of the latent displacement under degradation is this one term.**
(Stock-preproc row recorded explicitly, as you asked: it is the "\|dz\| stock" column.)

### Feature-level shift, no encoder involved, 2048 events
Mean |shift| of a surviving candidate's pt feature (nats):

| severity | stock (sum_pt) | AbsPt | MaxPt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0.1730 | 0.0000 | 0.0720 |
| 0.4 | 0.3810 | 0.0000 | 0.1505 |
| 0.6 | 0.6469 | 0.0000 | 0.2549 |
| 0.8 | 1.0123 | 0.0000 | 0.3961 |

`AbsPt` is exactly invariant by construction. `MaxPt` moves about 2.5x less than stock — it only moves
when the *leading* candidate is dropped, instead of a little for every candidate lost.
