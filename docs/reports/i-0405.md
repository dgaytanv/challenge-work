## A bug I caught before it produced a number, not after
My first driver decided "this arm is already trained" by testing **whether a checkpoint exists**. That is wrong:
`train.py` writes a best-so-far checkpoint after every improving epoch, so a run three epochs in already has
one. The driver skipped `i1p_ff256l1_s22` at **9 of 25 epochs** and would have benched it and reported the
result as that arm's row — a fully populated, plausible, wrong number, and the same shape as campaign 1's
stale-checkpoint pick. Fixed: completion is now the `] released` line `gpu_slot.sh` writes into the arm's own
log, and the bench step **refuses** an arm without it. This is why WP-H's warning about mtime globs is worth
generalising — the failure is not only "which file", it is "is this run finished at all".

Also hardened, same class: both my bench driver and my geometry tool now **assert exactly one primary
checkpoint** rather than taking the newest. `aux/` holds `_bestauc` and `_last`, and `_last` is written last, so
any mtime rule that ever sees `aux/` grades the wrong model silently. My globs are non-recursive so `aux/` was
already excluded — the assert makes that a checked property instead of a lucky one.

## First latent-geometry result (CPU, no slot) — answers WP-K's cross-package question
Seed 11 only, 4000 clean eval events. `ratio_mon` is the mean-of-norms convention campaign 1's 0.17× stock
reference uses; `ratio_rms` is RMS; `factor` is their ratio, computed per arm from the real latents.

| arm | latent_dim | params | offset | ratio_mon | ratio_rms | factor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I1′ ff256, 1 layer | 6 | 222,254 | 3.30 | **1.23** | 1.15 | 1.072 |
| I3 embed 256 | 6 | 343,046 | 10.54 | **2.21** | 2.07 | 1.063 |
| I4 seeds 8 | 6 | 94,214 | 12.36 | **3.28** | 3.11 | 1.054 |
| I6 latent 32 | 32 | 102,944 | 13.72 | **2.68** | 2.57 | 1.046 |

**Answer to "does a wider latent inflate the offset more": on this evidence, no.** The spread *across the
three latent_dim-6 arms* is 1.23 → 3.28, a factor of 2.7, and I6 at 2.68 sits inside it. At one seed the
architecture change dominates any latent-width effect. **Single seed; nothing claimed until all three land.**

*Corrected 04:10, before anyone read the first version.* I had written that I1′'s low ratio meant "a block
suppresses the offset inflation WP-K observes". That was wrong, and it was wrong because I built on a sentence
WP-K has since retracted. Their control run shows the **one-view champion does not inflate at all** — offset
flat at 3.3–3.6 while spread grows 0.44 → 1.51, settling near 2.36×. The inflation is specific to the
**two-view** configuration. Every arm of mine is one-view, so there is no inflation here for a block to
suppress; my 1.23–3.28 are ordinary levels around the champion's own 2.36×, not a rescue. What remains is that
capacity variants differ in resting ratio by 2.7×, which is worth reporting and is not a mechanism claim.

Caveat carried: both offset and spread are norms in the full latent space and grow with dimension, so a
dim-6-vs-dim-32 ratio comparison is confounded by dimension in a way I cannot currently separate. The
convention factor is itself dimension-dependent (1.072 at dim 6 vs 1.046 at dim 32 here, measured), so which
convention is quoted shifts the two groups by different amounts — ~3% — in the same direction as the
hypothesis. Both columns are reported for that reason.
