## Why n is small, stated plainly
1. **Campaign-wide bench deadlock 04:13–04:31** (WP-H's token-before-slot bug). Every bench on the box stalled.
2. **My queued benches had to be drained and relaunched** afterwards, because they were running the old
   scheduler from a deleted inode.

**Correction, made against myself in the other direction.** An earlier version of this section said I had
killed two of my own *running* benches at 04:29 and destroyed ~8 minutes of finished work. That was wrong.
`gpu.log` shows my first bench acquisition was at **04:50:44** — nothing of mine had acquired a slot before
04:31, so the two benches I killed were QUEUED in the deadlock and had never started. I inferred they were
running because `nvidia-smi` showed nothing after my kill; the emptiness was the deadlock, not my kill.
WP-H then tested the underlying claim four ways and found that an acquired job survives a plain `kill`, a
group TERM and `pkill -f`, and dies only to SIGKILL on the process group. So the guarantee they documented
holds, my kill destroyed nothing, and the honest cause of the thin table is the deadlock plus queue depth.
I had asserted a self-blaming cause from an inference without checking the log that would have settled it.

Measured bench cost once acquired is **~70–150 s**, not the ~14 min I estimated from wall clock — the rest
was queue time.

**I6's effective rank is ~2 out of 32, on all three seeds.** Widening the latent from 6 to 32 bought about a
third of an effective direction; its condition number is unpopulated directions, not conditioning.

**MY MECHANISM REASONING FROM THAT WAS WRONG, AND I6'S BENCH IS THE EVIDENCE.** I recorded, before any bench
returned, that I6 would not separate *because* latent width is not the binding constraint — no arm uses even
two effective directions out of six. I6 then benched at **0.8291, the highest single-seed row in my package**.
The literal prediction is not yet falsified, because with n=1 nothing separates from anything. But the
reasoning behind it predicted I6 would be unremarkable or worst, and it is the best.

The caveat I attached to the prediction is what defeats it, and I attached it precisely because campaign 1 had
already measured this: **variance is not discriminability.** The 26 near-null directions carry ~3% of the
variance and effective rank is blind to whether the probe can use them. A spectrum tells you how the latent
SPREADS; it cannot tell you where the discriminative direction points. I should treat effective rank as a
description of the latent and not as a predictor of the metric — which is exactly the conclusion I pressed on
WP-K about their condition number an hour earlier, and then failed to apply to my own diagnostic.

**A cross-package result that cost nothing:** the champion's condition number on the real data regime is **78**,
not the 6,000–18,000 WP-K measured under `--test_mode`. That retired WP-K's conditioning lead, and saved them
two GPU slots they had queued to measure it.

### What adding seeds actually did — a claim of mine, corrected
| arm | seeds | n=1 | n=2 | n=3 | range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| I1′ | 0.8286 / 0.8304 / 0.8273 | 0.8286 | 0.8295 ↑ | 0.8288 ↓ | 0.0031 |
| I3 | 0.8262 / 0.8282 | 0.8262 | 0.8272 ↑ | — | 0.0020 |
| I4 | 0.8284 / 0.8256 | 0.8284 | 0.8270 ↓ | — | 0.0028 |
| I6 | 0.8291 / 0.8294 / 0.8289 | 0.8291 | 0.8293 ↑ | 0.8292 ↓ | **0.0006** |

**Correction.** I reported to the planner that "every seed added moved an arm DOWN, except I6's", and asked
for it to be recorded as the concrete instance of the multiplicity point. **It is false.** Two of four arms
went UP on their second seed, and I1′ went up before it came down — which I had described correctly in the
same message and then generalised straight past. I pattern-matched on the two arms I had been advocating for,
which happen to be the two that fell, and did not check the sentence against my own table.

**The accurate statement:** adding a seed moved an arm's mean by up to 0.0014 in **either** direction. There
is no systematic drift. What the data shows is that a **single-seed point estimate is unreliable at the scale
of the effects being hunted** — I4 moved 0.0028 between its two seeds, I1′ 0.0031 across three, I3 0.0020
across two, each comparable to or larger than that arm's entire margin over the reference. That is the
multiplicity point, and it does not need the directional claim, which would also license the opposite error:
treating a survived second seed as confirmation.

I6 remains the exception on SPREAD, not on direction: its three seeds span 0.0006 against every other arm's
0.0020–0.0031. Still a hypothesis, still needs many more than three seeds.

### A correction I made that was itself wrong
I briefly reported that `i3_embed256_s22` never completed and that the latent-geometry file was contaminated
by a partial checkpoint. **Both claims were false.** That run completed normally (25 epochs, `released rc=0` at
04:26:32) and every geometry row is valid.

Cause: two processes wrote to that arm's log after my duplicate launch, leaving it full of NUL bytes (~64,000
of 84,281). **`grep` silently treats such a file as binary and prints nothing at all — not "0" — and exits 1.**
I read that emptiness as "these lines were never written". `grep -a` shows them immediately, and my own Python
guard (`errors='ignore'`) had read the file correctly all along — I concluded the tool was wrong when it was
right. Fourth instance tonight of a tool returning a confident-looking nothing, and my second.

**An empty result is not a finding until the tool has been shown to return a non-empty one under conditions
known to be true.** I3 therefore has **three completed seeds**; only two were benched, because I drained its
s22 bench by accident.

## Recommendation, against the pre-registered rule
**Nothing from WP-I is promotable.** Ties go to the smaller model (00-common-v2.md), and at 2.48× the
champion I1′ loses that clause outright unless it separates — which on one seed it does not. No arm has the three seeds the rule requires, and the one row I have is
inside seed noise. Under the rule as written — seed mean ≥ 0.828314, no separable regression, an independent
diagnostic — WP-I supplies no candidate.

If the planner has slots before the 05:25 stop, spend them getting ONE arm to n=3 rather than on more first
seeds. **That arm should be I4 or I6, not I1′** — a revision of my own earlier advice, made once I had I4's number.
I1′ 0.8286 and I4 0.8284 differ by 0.0002 against a seed std of 0.0030, so they are indistinguishable, and
**I4 is 1.05× the champion where I1′ is 2.48×**. "Ties go to the smaller model" decides between them and it
decides for I4. I recommended I1′ before I4 had benched, and the size clause was already in the rules when I
did; the earlier advice was premature rather than wrong on the evidence I had, but it was advice I should not
have given until the cheaper arm had reported.

## For WP-K's lever-versus-description test
I1′ has the WORST conditioning of my dim-6 arms (382–1951 against the champion's 78) and the LOWEST effective
rank (1.40–1.56), and it is the best-benching arm so far. On this one row that is evidence **against**
conditioning being a lever — consistent with WP-K's own recorded prediction. One seed; not a conclusion.
