## Q3 — I4 could not be a config kwarg, and the reason generalises
`train.py` reads an `encoder_kwargs` config key (WP-D added it), so `num_seeds: 8` *trains* fine. But
**`bench_eval.py:156` and `eval.py:34` both construct the encoder from the grader's fixed signature and pass
no keyword arguments**, so an 8-seed checkpoint would be untestable by our own bench and ungradable by
`eval.py`. It would have failed loudly here (`pma.seeds` is [1,8,E] against [1,4,E], `load_state_dict` raises)
— but relying on a failure being loud is what campaign 1's register says not to do. I made it a **class whose
default is the final choice**, `PMAEncoder8`, selected by `encoder_class:` like every other arm. If it is ever
promoted, the shipped branch aliases `TransformerEncoder = PMAEncoder8` and the grader gets 8 seeds from the
signature alone. **Any package adding an encoder option through `encoder_kwargs` has the same problem**, so
this may be worth a general note from you.

## The finding that may matter most: the capacity ladder has a hole, and it is where the answer probably lives
`I1` is 7.62× and `I2` is 14.24× because `TransformerEncoderBlock` hard-codes **`dim_feedforward=2048`** — 16×
the embed width — and `PMAEncoder` never passes it. So the specified ladder is 1.00, 1.05, 1.06, 1.15, 3.83,
**7.62**, 14.24: nothing at all between 1.15× and 3.83×, and the layer arms jump straight past it.

That matters because of what campaign 1 actually measured: a-pma at **27×** bought **+0.0022** on the bench and
**tied** on the official grader. The open question is not "does a lot more capacity help" — it is "how little
extra buys that +0.0022, if anything does". Narrowing the FFN reaches exactly the missing region (measured):

| `dim_feedforward` | 1 layer | 2 layers |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 | 189,358 (2.11×) | 289,110 (3.23×) |
| 256 | 222,254 (2.48×) | 354,902 (3.96×) |
| 512 | 288,046 (3.21×) | 486,486 (5.43×) |
| 2048 (current) | 682,798 (7.62×) | 1,275,990 (14.24×) |

A one-layer block at `dim_feedforward: 256` is **2.48×** the champion — a genuine middle rung, and cheaper than
I3 which is already on the list at 3.83×. It needs a small change inside my package boundary (plumb
`dim_feedforward` through `PMAEncoder` as a kwarg, defaulting to 2048 so nothing existing moves) plus a class
or config key the bench can see, exactly as in Q3. **I have not done this** — it would be a seventh arm when
Q2 suggests I may already have too many, and adding capacity knobs unasked is how a screening budget
disappears. Say the word and it replaces I2.
