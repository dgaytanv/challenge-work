## 1. PFPreProcessorMeanPt (E's rule, verified here)
`log(pt_i / mean_pt)` == `log(pt_i / sum_pt) + log(n_valid)`. Robust because a dead region removes
candidates and their pt together, so `sum_pt` and `n_valid` shrink by about the same factor.

Mean |shift| of a surviving candidate's pt feature (nats), 2048 events:

| severity | stock | AbsPt | MaxPt | MeanPt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0.1730 | 0.0000 | 0.0720 | **0.0171** |
| 0.4 | 0.3810 | 0.0000 | 0.1505 | **0.0282** |
| 0.6 | 0.6469 | 0.0000 | 0.2549 | **0.0395** |
| 0.8 | 1.0123 | 0.0000 | 0.3961 | **0.0517** |

95% of the stock shift gone at severity 0.8, ~8x better than MaxPt, and unlike AbsPt it keeps
relative-scale information. MeanPt is the front-runner.

### Caveat worth keeping (it failed a test of mine first)
Measuring the train/eval candidate-count offset by truncating a 400-slot event to its first 200 slots is
**misleading**: the data is pt-sorted, so the leading 200 carry ~66% of the pt but 50% of the count, and
`mean_pt` over-corrects. That route says MeanPt only reduces the offset 0.4091 -> 0.2841 (31%), versus
exactly 0 for AbsPt/MaxPt. On the real 200-candidate train file vs the real 400-candidate eval file —
the comparison that matches what the encoder actually sees — MeanPt wins outright:

| rule | train | eval | shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock | -5.3894 | -6.1204 | -0.7310 |
| AbsPt | 1.4602 | 1.2522 | -0.2081 |
| MaxPt | -1.7426 | -2.3021 | -0.5595 |
| MeanPt | -0.0911 | -0.1289 | **-0.0378** |

Shared bench, same checkpoint: `cells` family area **0.8347**, mean_area 0.7918.

**My eval-mode module reproduces the bench's `cells` family to 0.0006** — independent implementations,
2000 vs 20000 events, different severity grids. That is the intended cross-check and it passes.

Interpretation, stated plainly: my eval mode is a **single-family (coarse-cell) proxy**, so it reads about
0.04 *higher* than `mean_area`, which averages five families including the much harsher `rect` (0.757).
Nobody should quote my local sweep as a mean_area estimate. The bench stays the ruler.
