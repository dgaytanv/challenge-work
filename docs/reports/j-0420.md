## 1. train.py config hook (planner ruled GO)

`degradation_kwargs`, a dict under `hyperparameters:`, matching the existing `encoder_kwargs`
convention. It reaches anything in `Degradation.__init__` **except** `severity`, `rotate_phi`,
`reflect_eta` and `eta_max`, which `train.py` owns (`eta_max` keeps its own flat key). Setting one
of those, or any unknown key, raises — a silent no-op there would train a config that looks like an
arm and is actually the champion.

The two_view `symmetry` module deliberately does NOT receive the block. WP-K confirmed that is a
property their package depends on rather than a limitation: the symmetries are applied once before
the views split so the consistency term targets dead-region invariance alone, and a config key that
made view-specific symmetries possible by accident is the trap WP-C's writeup names.

**Verified bit-identical**: `champion.yaml` at 1 epoch, `--test_mode`, CPU, seed 11, run against the
parent commit and against the patched one. Same losses to six decimals and every tensor in the
checkpoint — encoder, preproc, projector, classifier, optimizer, scheduler, norm_constants —
compares equal under `torch.equal`. Both error paths exercised. Key names sent to WP-K and WP-L;
neither collides (WP-L is ~25 lines below me in the same config block, WP-K is ~20 below that).

## 2. J5 replaced J4, and it is the result I did not expect

The planner dropped J4 (curriculum) on my argument that its warm-up touches 8.5% of training and
would land under the pipeline floor, and replaced it with edge-aware placement of our five families.
Building it turned up **three** separate undershoot mechanisms, not one, each measured before being
fixed and each compounding with the others:

| mechanism | what it is | evidence it is real |
| --- | --- | --- |
| placement | `rect`/`strip` size dead regions by PLANE area, but the candidate density is edge-peaked in eta | 30% of candidates in `\|eta\| in [4,5]` against 20% for a uniform plane, hard-clipped at 5.000 |
| overlap | shapes are scaled so their areas SUM to `s`, then placed independently, so the union is smaller | closed-form union inversion moved `rect` from 0.146 to 0.111 mean abs error |
| p ceiling | a family killing with probability `p<1` inside its region cannot remove more than `E[p]` of an event, however wide the region | `strip` sat at exactly 0.746 and `wedge` at 0.798 at s=1.0 — their `E[p]` to three decimals |

Fixes: the eta extent now lives in the batch's **empirical eta-CDF coordinate**, computed per batch
so the same code is correct on the PF file and the L1T file with no stored constant; the union is
inverted rather than summed (`1-(1-a)^m` for bands, a 24-step bisection for `rect`, whose boxes are
heterogeneous and clipped at the eta edges — eta does not wrap, phi does); and `p` ramps to 1 with
severity, keeping the partial-kill character at low `s`.

Dropped fraction against requested severity, 4000 eval events, mean absolute error over
`s in {0.2 ... 1.0}`:

```
            champion    J5        s=0.2  s=0.4  s=0.6  s=0.8  s=1.0  (J5)
  rect        0.231   -> 0.028    0.200  0.399  0.592  0.759  0.908
  wedge       0.197   -> 0.011    0.196  0.387  0.581  0.780  1.000
  strip       0.212   -> 0.031    0.188  0.370  0.551  0.740  0.996
  cells       0.100      0.100    unchanged; 0.100 is its designed p_mean=0.65 ceiling, not an error
  towers      0.000      0.000    unchanged; already exact
```

`rect`'s residual 0.028 is honest and documented in the code: the bisection treats the k boxes as
independent, so it ignores overlap between the particular boxes drawn.

**The part worth arguing about.** J5 changes only how faithfully a family realises the severity it
is handed — it does not touch the severity sampler at all. Yet it moves the effective dropped
fraction from mean 0.248 to 0.295 and the fraction of events above 0.7 from **2.7% to 8.6%**. So
roughly a third of the effective-severity deficit that J2 was designed to attack was calibration
error rather than the sampler. J2 and J5 are independent levers on the same quantity and, if both
help, the natural Phase-2 arm is J2 built on J5's families rather than on the on-target three.

`on_target_placement=False` is the default and consumes no RNG; the champion's effective histogram
reproduces exactly (==0 23.4%, >0.7 2.7%, >0.85 0.1%, mean 0.248).

## 3. The four arms, each one change from its stated reference

| arm | change | reference | effective dropped fraction: ==0 / >0.7 / >0.85 / mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| champion | — | — | 23.4% / 2.7% / 0.1% / 0.248 |
| J1 | families 5 -> 10 (Group 3's five added) | champion | 23.8% / 5.5% / 0.2% / 0.270 |
| J2 | `p_high_severity` 0.0 -> 0.40 | **J1**, not the champion | 24.0% / **30.7%** / 13.9% / 0.408 |
| J3 | `p_clean` 0.15 -> 0.0 | champion | 10.2% / 3.3% / 0.1% / 0.291 |
| J5 | `on_target_placement` false -> true | champion | 23.4% / 8.6% / 1.3% / 0.295 |

J2 is one change from J1 because the high-severity component can only draw from families that exist;
compare J2 to J1, not to the champion. Its 30.7% is the planner's target of 30% of events above 0.7
**in effective dropped fraction**, which is the specification that matters — 30% nominal would have
delivered about 10% effective.

Two caveats I would rather state now than be asked about later:

- **J2's high component is ~2/3 thinning.** Two of the three on-target families (`towers` at cell
  0.1, `c_cand_loss`) are close to per-candidate thinning rather than a contiguous dead region, and
  only `c_phi_wedge` is a genuine large dead region. If J2 helps, which of those did it is not yet
  established. J5 makes our geometric families on-target and would let the high component be
  geometric; that is the Phase-2 follow-up.
- **J3 does not mean "every event is degraded".** ~10% of events still lose no candidate at all,
  because `p_pt_scale=0.10` applies the dead region as a pt rescale rather than by zeroing rows.
  Measured: 23.4% -> 10.2%, not to 0%. Planner ruled the pt-rescale branch stays.

**J1 uses ONLY Group 3's five TRAINING families.** Their held-out three — `ellipse`,
`cell_dropout`, `edge_truncation` — are not ported and must never be, because the colleague bench
suite is built from exactly those and training on them would destroy the only independent
generalisation check we have. Verified: the only mentions of those names in
`src/embedding/degradation.py` are the four comment lines saying so. Nor is anything resembling our
own held-out shapes (ellipse, annulus, diagonal).

One honest caveat for reading a colleague-suite result on J1: their held-out `cell_dropout` is a
Bernoulli field over a 10x12 grid, and our new `c_cand_loss` is independent Bernoulli **per
candidate** — the fine-granularity limit of the same idea. So J1 does partially anticipate one of
their three. It is a weak effect rather than contamination, because the champion already trains on
`towers`, which is a Bernoulli field at cell 0.1 and is nearer to per-candidate thinning than
`cell_dropout` is; J1 adds no new capability there, only more weight on it. State it when quoting
the colleague number, and quote their families individually rather than the suite mean (the reasons
are in `writeup/E-heldout-calibration.md`).

Configs `configs/c2/{j1,j2,j3,j5}_s{11,22,33}.yaml`, each with its measured effective histogram in

## 5. Two things I got wrong tonight

- I reported gpu_slot.sh's errors as an in-place edit shifting byte offsets under running shells.
  WP-H showed direct evidence it was an atomic replace (a running waiter's fd 255 points at
  `gpu_slot.sh (deleted)`, which only happens after a rename) and that the real cause was a
  TOCTOU race in their fair-share scan. My conclusion — don't relaunch, ordering is unaffected —
  was right, but for the wrong reason. Their check is the one to keep: `ls -l /proc/<pid>/fd | grep
  gpu_slot` on a live waiter settles it after the fact.
- The table above gives `rect`'s champion mean absolute error as 0.231 and the figure prints 0.229.
  Same quantity, same 4000 events, different RNG seed for the region draw, so the gap is sampling.
  The figure recomputes it from its own numbers on purpose, so it cannot go stale against the text;
  the cost is that the two will always differ in the third decimal.
