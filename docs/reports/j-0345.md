## 2. Dropped-fraction calibration of every family (the requested deliverable)

First, per campaign-1 register class 9 (*validate the reference before reporting an error against
it*), the target. Their `_spatial_mask` returns a plain boolean and `apply` does
`x.masked_fill(drop, 0.0)` — **no per-candidate probability anywhere**, so their `p_drop` is 1.0 and
the target for all five ported families is `s` itself. Confirmed by reading their source, not
assumed from my own comment.

Fraction of real candidates dropped, 4000 eval events, severity forced (not sampled):

`cells` is **not** miscalibrated: `_drop_cells_multi` uses `p_lo=0.3, p_hi=1.0`, so `frac = s/0.65`
saturates at 1 and the family's designed ceiling is `p_mean = 0.65`. It hits `min(s, 0.65)` to three
decimals. (Note this differs from the *bench* `cells`, whose `p_drop ~ U(0.5, 1.0)` gives target
`0.75s` — different code, same name. Campaign 1 made this exact mistake in the other direction.)

`towers`, `c_phi_wedge` and `c_cand_loss` land on target to ≤0.001 at every severity. Those three
are the families whose geometry is independent of the eta density (per-candidate Bernoulli; a phi
band, and phi is uniform), which is the clue to the rest.

**The newly ported families are better calibrated than three of our own five.** `c_rect` /
`c_eta_band` / `c_multi_patch` carry mean absolute error 0.08 / 0.05 / 0.06 against `s`;
`rect` / `wedge` / `strip` carry 0.23 / 0.20 / 0.21.

## 3. Why: the candidate density is edge-peaked in eta, not centre-peaked

Measured on the eval file (4k events, `pt > 0` only):

```
  frac |eta| < 1: 0.197    < 2: 0.342    < 3: 0.454    < 4: 0.699    < 4.5: 0.849    < 5: 0.999
  uniform on [-5,5] would give   0.200         0.400         0.600         0.800           0.900
  max |eta| = 5.000 exactly (the distribution is clipped at the acceptance edge)
```

30% of all candidates sit in `|eta| ∈ [4, 5]` and 15% in `[4.5, 5.0]`, against 20% and 10% for a
uniform plane. **Any family whose dead region is placed strictly inside the acceptance cannot reach
the band where the candidates actually are.** Directly confirmed — drop rate by `|eta|` bin, which
a family calibrated on plane area would make flat:

```
c_eta_band   s=0.4   0-1:0.669  1-2:0.590  2-3:0.414  3-4:0.251  4-4.5:0.127  4.5-5:0.042
             s=0.8   0-1:1.000  1-2:1.000  2-3:1.000  3-4:0.767  4-4.5:0.380  4.5-5:0.125
c_rect       s=1.0   0-1:0.999  1-2:0.985  2-3:0.932  3-4:0.840  4-4.5:0.716  4.5-5:0.596
```

The `c_*` families constrain their centre to `[-eta_max + w/2, eta_max - w/2]` (their own code, kept
verbatim), so at `s=0.8` `c_eta_band` erases the entire core and leaves seven eighths of the forward
region alive. Our `_drop_bands` samples centres over the full span and clips instead, so it has no
anti-correlation — its undershoot is overlap-of-bands and width clamping, a different and milder
mechanism, but on the same side.

I am **not** proposing to "fix" the placement yet. The shapes' value here is that they are an
independently designed geometry; recentring them would make them ours. Flagging it as a decision.

## 4. The finding that matters: nominal severity is not effective severity

Earlier I measured the champion's **nominal** severity histogram by simulating 7025 steps of a
25-epoch run: mean `s` 0.349, 13.9% of mass above 0.7, **0.0% above 0.85** (`s_max`), curriculum
warm-up over after 600 of 7025 steps = 8.5% of training.

Running the real `_forward_train` at the post-curriculum state and counting rows actually zeroed
gives the **effective** distribution (8192 events, champion settings exactly as `train.py` builds
them):

```
   0.0-0.1  35.9%     0.4-0.5  11.3%     0.8-0.9   1.0%
   0.1-0.2  12.3%     0.5-0.6   7.7%     0.9-1.0   0.0%
   0.2-0.3  11.8%     0.6-0.7   5.5%
   0.3-0.4  12.7%     0.7-0.8   1.7%

  exactly 0 (no candidate lost)   23.4%    <- configured p_clean is 0.15
  mass above 0.7                   2.7%    <- nominal said 13.9%; J2 target is 30%
  mass above 0.85                  0.1%
  mean effective drop             0.248    <- nominal mean severity 0.349
```

Two consequences:

1. **The champion has essentially never seen an event lose more than 70% of its candidates** (2.7%
   of steps), while the grader sweeps severity to 1.0 and the bench families remove 0.94-1.00 of
   candidates at `s=1.0` (`E-heldout-calibration.md`). The gap between the training and scoring
   regimes is much larger than the nominal `s_max = 0.85` suggested.
2. **J2 must be specified in effective dropped fraction, not nominal `s`.** Raising nominal mass
   above 0.7 to 30% with the current families would land near 10% effective. The cheapest fix that
   actually moves the effective tail is to include the on-target families (`towers`, `c_cand_loss`,
   `c_phi_wedge`) in the high-severity part of the mixture, since only those reach where they aim.

The 23.4% "exactly 0" against a configured `p_clean = 0.15` is the `p_pt_scale = 0.10` branch: a
tenth of events have their dead region applied as a pt rescale, so no row is zeroed. Real
no-dead-region fraction is ~25%, not 15%. This matters for J3's `p_clean` sweep — setting
`p_clean = 0.0` still leaves ~10% of events with no candidate removed.

## 5. J4 (curriculum) — a prediction, not yet a result

The warm-up covers 600 of ~7025 steps, 8.5% of training, and ramps `s_max` from 0.2 to 0.85.
Removing or doubling it changes the severity seen by <9% of steps. I expect J4 to be at or below the
pipeline reproducibility floor (~0.002 between independently trained full models,
`E-probe-noise-floor.md`), i.e. a no-op that cannot be resolved by a single pair of runs. I will
still run it, but it should be scheduled *after* J2/J3 and its likely verdict should be "not
separable", not "no effect".
