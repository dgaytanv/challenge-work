## The J1 story, because it is the one that looked real

Matched-seed deltas, which is the stronger design (it removes the initialisation draw):

```
  seed 11   j1 0.8296   ref 0.8250 / 0.8255 (same checkpoint, twice)   +0.0044
  seed 22   j1 0.8266   ref 0.8296                                     -0.0030
  seed 33   j1 0.8268   ref 0.8239                                     +0.0029
```

The sign flips at seed 22 and comes back at seed 33. Mean matched-seed delta +0.0014 with a spread
of 0.0040 across three seeds, so `3*sigma/sqrt(3)` = 0.0069 against a mean of 0.0014: even the
matched-seed design, which removes the initialisation draw, cannot separate them. Per-family at seed 11, which is what made it persuasive:

```
  family   ref s11   j1 s11    delta
  rect      0.7596   0.7734   +0.0138     <- essentially all of the gain
  strip     0.8046   0.8092   +0.0046
  cells     0.8739   0.8757   +0.0018
  wedge     0.8582   0.8600   +0.0018
  towers    0.8286   0.8296   +0.0010
  clean     0.9228   0.9215   -0.0013
```

That is exactly where the mechanism predicts: `rect` was the champion's worst scoring family, and
`c_rect` and `c_multi_patch` are the axis-aligned boxes J1 adds, drawn from a different prior than
ours. I called it "mechanistically coherent" and argued for the arm on it.

**It was coherent and it was still one seed.** A single seed produces a per-family breakdown
whatever the truth is, and some family always carries the largest delta; finding a story for
whichever one it is costs nothing. The coherence made one draw feel like evidence. WP-M raised the
same objection at 04:55 — that +0.0045 at matched seed is one run against one run, and the
reference's own two benches of the same checkpoint already differ by 0.0005 — and was right.

## J2 and J5 failed in informative directions

**J2** (30.7% of events losing more than 0.7 of their candidates, against the champion's 2.7%) lost
**0.0086 of clean AUC** (0.9142 against 0.9220) and gained no robustness (0.8240). Every scoring
family fell. Training a much harder corruption regime cost representation quality and bought
nothing at the severities that are scored. This is the clearest single result WP-J produced: the
champion's severity distribution is not obviously too soft, and the case for pushing it harder —
which the effective-severity measurement made look compelling — does not survive contact.

**J5** (the calibration fix: `rect` 0.231 -> 0.028 mean absolute error against requested severity,
`wedge` 0.197 -> 0.011, `strip` 0.212 -> 0.031) benched **0.8251**, inside the seed floor of the
reference. The generator was materially miscalibrated, the miscalibration is real and measured, and
**fixing it did not move the score.** That is worth stating plainly: a correct diagnosis of a real
defect is not the same as a defect that mattered.

## Suites on J1 seed 11, and a prediction that held

Held-out (ours) and colleague (Group 3's held-out three), both against the champion at matched
seed 11:

```
  held-out        champion          j1-s11      delta
  ellipse         0.7937 / 0.7935   0.7940     +0.0004
  annulus         0.8131 / 0.8128   0.8153     +0.0023
  diagonal        0.8054 / 0.8053   0.8066     +0.0013

  colleague       champion              j1-s11            delta
  c_ellipse         0.8296 / 0.8293     0.8313 ±0.0009   +0.0017 / +0.0020
  c_cell_dropout    0.8052 / 0.8048     0.8054 ±0.0010   +0.0002 / +0.0006
  c_edge_truncation 0.7633 / 0.7638     0.7617 ±0.0009   -0.0016 / -0.0021
```

**J1 trained only on Group 3's five TRAINING families and never on their held-out three**, so the
colleague suite is a real generalisation check for it — a same-design-family comparison, not a leak.
Verified: the only mentions of `ellipse`, `cell_dropout` and `edge_truncation` in
`src/embedding/degradation.py` are four comment lines saying they must never be ported.

Before seeing these numbers I told WP-M I expected any colleague effect on `c_ellipse` — a filled
convex region, closest in kind to the boxes J1 adds — rather than on `c_cell_dropout` or
`c_edge_truncation`. That is what happened. The `c_ellipse` gap clears probe noise
(`3*sqrt(0.0009²/5 + 0.0005²/5)` = 0.0014 against a gap of 0.0017) and is **far inside the seed
floor**, since these are two independently trained checkpoints. So it is a correct prediction about a
difference that is not established. It belongs in the writeup as a mechanism note, not as a claim.

One caveat stays attached, per WP-M: `c_cand_loss` is independent Bernoulli per candidate, the
fine-granularity limit of their `cell_dropout`, so that row is the weakened check and `c_ellipse` is
the clean test. The champion already trains on `towers` (a Bernoulli field at cell 0.1), so J1 adds
weight there rather than a new capability.

## What WP-J leaves behind that is not a score

- `degradation_kwargs` in `train.py`: the generator is now configurable, verified bit-identical by
  default (every checkpoint tensor equal under `torch.equal`), with owned and unknown keys raising.
- The measurement that the training generator's `severity` and the *fraction of candidates removed*
  are different quantities on this data, by up to 0.47 absolute for `rect` at s=1.0, with three
  distinct mechanisms identified and fixed. `writeup/J-generator-coverage.md`.
- The effective-severity histograms for the champion and every arm, in each config header.
- Two inspected figures, `runs/plots/j1_colleague_families` and `runs/plots/j5_placement`.
- The negative results above, which are the useful part: harder is not better (J2), and better
  calibrated is not better (J5).
