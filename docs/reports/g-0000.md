## Finding 1 — two EXACT restructurings, 33% of the parameters gone for free
`tests/test_restructure.py`, verified to **1.7e-5** on the same 2000 events, clean and degraded:
* **R1**: the 4 seed queries are constant parameters (num_layers=0), so `q = q_proj(seeds)` is a compile-time
  constant and `q_proj` + `k_proj` **fuse into one Dense 128 -> 32**:
  `A[(h,s),:] = sum_d q_flat[s,h*D+d] * W_k[h*D+d,:] / sqrt(D)`, `c[(h,s)]` likewise from `b_k`.
* **R2**: `h = phi(x) * keep` is redundant — the masked softmax already gives dead tokens weight 0, and
  `out = sum_n attn[n] v[n]`, so `v[dead]` never reaches the output.

**89,606 -> 60,198 parameters (-29,408, -33%) at zero accuracy cost, before any quantization.**
JSON: `quant/g2_restructure_test.json`.

## Finding 2 — ruling 2 check done, and it nearly bit me
A dead token does **not** carry zero after R2, it carries `phi(0)`, so its score is a fixed 32-vector
`A.phi(0) + c` measured at **[-2.79, +14.03]**. Live scores span **[-20.53, +59.07]** (20k events, severities
0.0/0.5/1.0 — the range is severity-independent to 1e-2). In the worst of the 32 channels the best LIVE score
is only **+0.35** above the dead score, so the entire safety margin is the bias itself, not the score geometry.

At the original MASK_BIG=32 the worst gap was **-32.35**: one dead token carries exp(-32.35)=8.9e-15 of the
best live token's weight, 400 of them carry 3.6e-12. Safe, but only 32 e-folds and nothing in QAT constrains
scores from growing. **MASK_BIG raised to 64**, which costs exactly ONE integer bit of score range
(7 -> 8, range becomes [-84.5, +59.1]) and doubles the margin. Restructure test re-run at 64: still 1.7e-5.
Added `assert_mask_margin()`, which recomputes the gap and **raises** below 20 e-folds after allowing for
`n_tokens` dead tokens; it will be run after every QAT run and its number reported.
JSON: `quant/g2_score_range_float_PF.json`.

## Finding 3 — ruling 4, dxysig: the BatchNorm already tames it; 3 integer bits are the real cost
Raw dxysig reaches 6.5e4, but the preprocessor's BatchNorm divides by a std that the same tail inflates, so
the **encoder input** sees |max| 65.3 (L1T) / 68.8 (PF), not 6.5e4. Per-channel integer bits of the 14 encoder
inputs (BN stats computed from each train split, gamma=1 beta=0, 20k events):

| channel | max abs | p99.9 | ratio | i_bits | i_bits at p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pt | 12.3 | 6.96 | 1.8 | 5 | 4 |
| dxy | 22.8 | 22.15 | **1.0** | 6 | 6 |
| **dxysig** | 65.3 | 13.74 | **4.8** | **8** | **5** |
| eta/phi | 1.8/1.7 | — | 1.0 | 2 | 2 |

So on L1T dxysig costs **3 integer bits for 0.1% of candidates** — at a fixed word length its fractional
resolution is 8x coarser than 99.9% of the data needs. PF is the same picture (8 -> 6, 2 bits).
**dxy is NOT an outlier problem** (ratio 1.00): 6 integer bits are genuinely required, because tanh(dxy)
saturates and the BN std is tiny. Squashing dxysig would recover 3 bits; I am not doing it unilaterally
(ruling 4) and it is only worth ordering a second D run if 3 bits move the bench. JSONs:
`quant/g2_input_range_L1T.json`, `quant/g2_input_range_PF.json`.

## Finding 4 — FOUR of the 14 input channels are constants (bigger than the dxysig question)
Measured over 20k events of both files: **pdgId 130, 1 and 2 never occur**, so those three one-hot channels
are **identically zero in both PF and L1T**. On L1T `is_pf` is additionally **constant 1.0**.
A constant input channel folds into the next layer's bias **exactly** (ruling 3's argument, generalised):
* zero channels: drop the column, no bias change;
* is_pf = 1: drop the column, add its weight column to the bias.

So the first Dense is exactly **14 -> 10 on L1T** and **14 -> 11 on PF**, a 29% cut of that layer's multiplies
with no retraining and no accuracy change. Will be applied at export and verified by the acceptance test.

## LayerNorm -> frozen affine without fine-tuning is catastrophic (kept as the justification)
max abs d = **6796 on a latent of scale 12** (rms 584). LayerNorm is per-sample; no population affine imitates
it. Hence QBatchNormalization trained with batch statistics, frozen and foldable at export.
Caveat recorded: keras BN cannot exclude dead rows from its batch statistics, so the running stats are taken
over live and dead tokens together. Cost of that shows up in G3 rather than being assumed away.
