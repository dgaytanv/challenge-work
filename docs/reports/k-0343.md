**CORRECTED.** The original claim here was "the spread is flat at 0.183 +- 0.002 while the offset
falls 1000-fold, so the penalty removes offset rather than inflating the denominator". The
flatness was the wrong-column artefact above. The true spread RISES about 31% across the scan
(0.221 -> 0.289 between lambda 0 and 0.01) while the offset falls 182-fold, so roughly 95% of the
log-scale ratio improvement is offset reduction and 5% is denominator growth. The direction of the
original conclusion survives; its supporting number did not.

A second correction, from WP-I measuring K1 against the champion on the same instrument
(4000 clean eval events): offset 9.508 -> 0.275 (34.6x) but spread 3.377 -> 0.105 (32.2x), with
the ratio moving only 7%. So between the CHAMPION and K1 the whole latent shrinks ~32x and the
offset comes down with it. My guard tested whether the ratio was bought by inflating the
denominator; it does not test the mirror case of shrinking numerator and denominator together.
What survives that check is scale-invariant: cond 78.2 -> 32.4 and effective rank 1.68 -> 1.94.

Caveat I am flagging against my own table: a batch-of-256 offset has a sampling floor of about
spread/sqrt(256) ~ 0.011 (a cloud with TRUE offset 0 reports 0.027 under this scheme). So the
bottom two rows read "at or below what this measurement can resolve", not "0.06x". The top rows
are far above the floor. WP-I's eval-set tool has no such floor and is the better instrument for
the level; mine is the trajectory.

## A campaign-1 attribution that does not survive
The register and WP-C's writeup attribute the offset inflation to the uncentred cosine being the
only term that rewarded it. **The lambda=0 control has no cosine term at all** — `consistency_weight: 0`,
raw MSE only — and the ratio still climbs 3.6x -> 64.3x in 8 epochs. The offset is a property of
the champion's architecture-plus-objective; the cosine was at most an accelerant. This needs
changing in the shared register and in the memory file, because the old sentence is currently
doing work in other people's reasoning.

## A silent failure of my own, logged
`torch.linalg.eigvalsh` has no CUDA half kernel, so under autocast the condition number raised,
a bare `except: cond = 0.0` swallowed it, and the **entire first scan logged a plausible 0.0**.
That is register class 4 — "not computed" wearing the clothes of "computed and fine" — reproduced
by me within an hour, in the diagnostic the planner had just singled out as the one campaign 1
lacked. Now float32 with autocast disabled; a genuine failure logs once and reports NaN, which
cannot be mistaken for a measurement. I also nearly picked lambda from a mid-run snapshot that
showed lambda=0.01 stopping at 4 epochs; it had simply not finished.
