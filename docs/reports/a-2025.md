## The headline: cos(z_clean, z_degraded) is a near-perfect stand-in for probe AUC

Every work package is built on the assumption that `mean_area` is driven by per-event latent
drift under the frozen clean probe. It was an assumption. It is now measured, on the stock
checkpoint, 8000 events, all five bench families x five severities
(`tests/latent_drift_diagnostic.py`, read-only measurement — nothing trains on the bench maps).

```
family    r(cos,AUC)  r(drift,AUC)  spearman  cos@s=1.0
rect          +0.886        -0.887    +1.000      0.102
wedge         +0.852        -0.815    +1.000      0.100
strip         +0.936        -0.935    +1.000      0.120
towers        +0.884        -0.855    +1.000      0.100
cells         +0.975        -0.959    +1.000      0.281
ALL 25        +0.850        -0.824    +0.985
```

**Within a family the rank correlation is exactly 1.000 in all five cases.** Cosine orders the
severities precisely as probe AUC does, every time. Across all 25 points spearman is +0.985.

**Recommendation: yes, log `cos(z_clean, z_degraded)` on validation.** It needs no probe
training, no eval set, and no bench — one extra forward pass on an already-degraded batch — and
it tracks the thing we are actually scored on. For WP-C it is close to a direct readout of the
consistency objective.

### The honest caveat: it ranks severities, not families
Across families the rank correlation drops to **+0.700**. Cosine orders the families
`cells > wedge > towers > strip > rect`; AUC orders them `cells > strip > wedge > towers > rect`
— wedge and strip swap. So use it as a *within-run, over-time* signal ("is my training making
the latent more stable"), **not** to predict `mean_area` or to decide which family is worst.
Any claim of the form "cosine went up by X so mean_area will go up by Y" is not supported by
this data; the mapping is monotone but not linear (Pearson +0.850 vs Spearman +0.985).

### Two physics observations worth having in the writeup
- `cells` is the outlier family in every column: it is the most robust (area 0.824) *and* the
  only family whose latent survives full severity (cos 0.281 at s=1.0 vs ~0.10 for everything
  else). Fine-grained scattered holes preserve the event's global structure; a single large
  contiguous hole destroys it.
- At s=1.0 for rect/wedge/towers the latent is essentially decorrelated from the clean one
  (cos ~ 0.10) and AUC sits at chance (0.454-0.506). Past that point there is no signal left to
  protect — the score is won in the 0.2-0.8 range, not at the endpoint.
