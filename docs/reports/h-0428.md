## Scheduler: final state

`gpu_slot.sh [--slots N] [--kind train|bench] [--priority] [--abandon-if-launcher-dies] cmd...`
plus `--queue` and `--drain <pid|all>`.

- Queued jobs **survive** their launcher (default). `--abandon-if-launcher-dies` restores the old rule.
- **Fair share**: on a free slot, the waiting job whose *package* holds fewest running slots wins;
  ties by queue time. Queueing more jobs cannot win a package more share.
- **`--priority`** beats fair share; allow-list `c2-h`/`c2-m` plus the planner-only grant file
  `locks/priority_grants`. Every priority acquisition logs `PRIORITY(pkg=...)`.
- **Benches may use any free slot** (04:26 ruling). **3-bench token cap RETAINED** — see below.
- **A job drained while queued exits 75**, not 0.

### Cap decision (mine, per the 04:26 ruling) — retained, with evidence
```
load average 9.96 on 8 cores      bench duration: n=21, median 59 s, max 93 s
top CPU: 3 non-scheduler jobs at ~100% each (Vitis clang-tidy, quant scripts)
```
Benches are **not crawling** — 59 s median. The cap is not currently binding (most waiters are
training jobs). Retaining it costs nothing now and protects against a late bench burst taking all
five slots away from arms still finishing, on a box already at ~10 load with three CPU hogs
outside the scheduler. I will lift it if benches start queueing behind it.

### Bugs found and fixed tonight (all mine)
1. Queued jobs silently reaped with their launcher — cost WP-L four runs.
2. `--abandon-if-launcher-dies` would have abandoned *every* detached job (setsid reparents to
   init before `$PPID` is read; `kill -0 1` always fails for non-root).
3. Bench cap by counting was a TOCTOU race — 5 benches passed a cap of 3. Now token locks.
4. Token released by `cleanup_q` right after acquisition, so the cap held only while *queued*.
5. Vanishing-registry race in **three** sites (`my_turn`, `running_for`, `running_benches`,
   `train_queued`). I fixed one and shipped believing I had fixed the class.
6. `--drain` reported **rc=0** — an EXIT trap ending in `return 0` laundered SIGTERM into success.
   A killed bench read as a completed one: a missing row that looks present. (Found by WP-L.)

## Retraction (mine, campaign 1)
Recomputing my campaign-1 claims against the seed floor: single-run arms need
`3·√(2s²)` = 0.0128, not the probe-refit thresholds I used.
`a-pma vs d-pma0-aug` (+0.0113, "the transformer body helps") goes from 2.2× separable to
**0.88× — not separable**. That was my headline finding. Retracted in the rulings log.
I used the variance of *refitting a probe on a fixed model* to judge a difference produced by
*training two different models* — and spent that evening correcting other people's thresholds
three times without asking whether I was dividing the right sigma by three at all.
