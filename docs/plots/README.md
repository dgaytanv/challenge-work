## Reading them

- **The held-out demotion rule compares candidate against candidate, not a candidate against its own scoring area** (planner's ruling, 22:33). Two of the three held-out families are degenerate at s=1.0: `dropped_fraction` is exactly 1.000 for ellipse and diagonal, so every candidate embeds identically there and sits at AUC 0.500 by construction, exactly as towers s=1.0 does on the scoring bench. Only annulus (0.95) carries information at full severity. That depresses every held-out mean by about 0.02 relative to its scoring mean_area, so read literally the rule would demote every candidate at once — which is a property of the family set, not of any candidate.
- **Three corruption suites, never mixed.** `curves` is the five scoring families (`bench_eval.py`); `heldout` is our generalisation check (ellipse, annulus, diagonal); `colleague` is colleague-group3's suite (ellipse, cell_dropout, edge_truncation). A run enters the scoring figure, the ranking and the accent only if its families are a subset of the scoring five — an allow-list, so a suite added upstream stays out of the scoring figures by default rather than silently polluting them. Suite is decided by the tag suffix first, because `ellipse` is a family name in two suites and the two are different implementations.
- **`colleague_latest` quotes per-family areas and draws no suite mean.** Their `c_edge_truncation` keeps `eta <= -3+6s OR eta >= 3-6s` (E), so the two half-planes overlap from s = 0.5 and the family becomes "drop nearly everything, chosen by a per-event coin flip" rather than edge truncation. Read that panel below s = 0.6 only, and do not average a family that stops being itself halfway through the sweep into a single number.
- **`curves` and `official` are different corruptions and are never put on one axis.** `curves` is our five-family development bench (`bench_eval.py`, the ruler we steer on); `official` is the organisers' 10×10 grid, marked TEMPORARY upstream and stated to change before judging. Nothing is tuned to it.
- **The bench and the official metric disagree, and the ruling follows the official one.** `a-pma` separably leads `mean_area` at R=20 (ratio 2.56) and held-out area (1.63); the official areas tie. The submission is `d-pma0-aug-meanpt` on the pre-registered official-margin rule, the completed certification chain and the 27.3x size difference, recorded as a disagreement between the proxy metric and the official one. Nothing in these figures should be read as the certified submission also winning the bench: it does not.
- **The FPGA line's stage A is not a quantization result.** `g-stageA-l1t` is R1+R2 (algebraic restructurings, exact to 1.7e-5) plus LayerNorm→BatchNorm (not exact, and forced: HGQ2 has no quantized LayerNorm) plus 12 epochs of distillation. Its **−0.0039 ± 0.0007** against `d-pma0-aug-meanpt-l1t` (ratio 3.89, separable) is the first measured price of that swap — a deliverable, not a regression. Caveat from its author: stage A's BatchNorm running statistics are taken over live AND dead tokens together, because keras `BatchNormalization` cannot exclude dead rows, so an unseparated part of that 0.0039 is the statistics rather than the swap.
- **Quantization itself costs nothing measurable, at the right beta.** `gB-q-b1e-6-l1t` (QuantizedPMAEncoder, beta 1e-6) benches 0.807930 ± 0.001125 against stage A's 0.807842 ± 0.000660: diff **+0.000088**, threshold 0.001751, ratio 0.05 — a tie, and its clean AUC is 0.0043 HIGHER. So on this evidence the cost of the FPGA line is the LayerNorm→BatchNorm swap (−0.0039, separable), not the quantization (0.000, tied).
- **There is no beta axis.** WP-G measured EBOPs identical to seven significant figures across beta0 = 0, 1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4 and 1e-3, so no claim of the form "at the right beta" is measurable, successful or otherwise. Beta has been replaced by a direct cap on the learned bit widths, which does produce a real curve (3 bit: EBOPs 6.06e7, distill rms 3.00; 4 bit: 1.84e8, 1.69; 6 bit: 3.81e8, 0.366; ~8 bit: 5.06e8, 0.265); those rows are benching now and are the honest accuracy-versus-bits axis. _An earlier version of this section read the quantization result as "free at the right beta", with a collapsed row beside it as the counter-case. Both halves were wrong: beta is not an axis, and the collapsed row was a harness artifact (below)._
- `gB-q-b1e-5-relu-l1t` is **withdrawn by its author and excluded from every figure**. Its 0.5226 was not ReLU failing: the activation was a constructor kwarg, `bench_eval.py` builds the encoder from `eval.py`'s signature and passes no kwargs, so 81 ReLU-trained tensors were strict-loaded into a GELU graph. Every tensor name and shape matched, nothing raised, and near-chance AUC in every family is exactly what that produces — while its distillation rms was a healthy 0.269. A figure cannot tell a real failure from a harness artifact, so a plotted failure carries an interpretation only with its author's confirmation.
- **The quantization knee is between 4 and 6 bits, and at 6 it is free.** WP-G's bit-cap ladder against stage A (all L1T, eta_max 3, R=5, seeded from stage A): cap 3 0.5000 (collapsed), cap 4 0.7797 ± 0.0018 (−0.028, separable), cap 6 0.8080 ± 0.0001 (+0.0002, tied), cap 8 0.8079 ± 0.0011 (tied), cap 10 0.8087 ± 0.0003 (tied). Everything at or above 6 bits is indistinguishable from the unquantized float-BN model. Cap 6 is the operating point: same accuracy as cap 8 for 3.81e8 EBOPs against 5.06e8. `GELU→ReLU` is also free (0.8078 ± 0.0009, diff 0.000013).
- **Do not write that ReLU beats GELU.** The measured operating point `gF-bits6-relu-l1t` (6-bit cap + ReLU, 0.8089 ± 0.0005) is +0.00104 against unquantized stage A (threshold 0.00115, TIED, nominally above) and +0.00086 against the 6-bit GELU row — which is INSIDE the 0.0008 pipeline reproducibility below. Every row at cap ≥ 6 is mutually tied on accuracy; 6-bit ReLU is chosen on cost (25% fewer EBOPs than cap 8/10) and on needing no lookup table at all. It was run as a combination because "6-bit with ReLU" had only ever been composed from a 6-bit GELU row and a cap-8 ReLU row, and this project's own ruling records that ablation arms are not additive.
- **Distillation loss and `mean_area` are not monotonically related** (WP-G). The masked-BN arm `gE-stageA-maskedbn-l1t` matches the float teacher BETTER than stage A (distill rms 0.3682 against 0.3889) while scoring WORSE on the ruler (0.8044 against 0.8078), and it does not recover the LayerNorm→BatchNorm cost. So if the suspicious-collapse check above ever fires on a distilled row, it means "look at this", never "this is broken".
- **The whole train-and-bench pipeline adds no variance of its own** (WP-G). Cap 8 and cap 10 are the same configuration — the 10-bit cap never binds — trained twice and benched twice: 0.8079 ± 0.0011 and 0.8087 ± 0.0003, a spread of 0.0008. That sits at the per-refit probe floor rather than above it, so the error bars on these figures are probe-refit noise and not training noise. It is a free calibration of the axis every one of these figures is drawn on.
- The 3-bit collapse is REAL, not an artifact: over 500 eval events the encoder emits ONE distinct latent with per-dimension std exactly 0.0000, and AUC 0.5000 with zero variance is the correct answer to a constant encoder. The zero variance IS the signature that distinguishes it from the withdrawn harness artifact, which had healthy per-event variation. Its EBOPs figure is the cost of a dead network and is not an operating point.
- On the L1T eval file the ladder is `d-pma0-aug-meanpt-l1t` 0.8117, `g-stageA-l1t` 0.8078, `winner-l1t-eta3` 0.8063, `winner-l1t` 0.8046. So the hardware-shaped, LayerNorm-free, 60,198-parameter model still sits **above** the PF-trained competition winner evaluated on the same file — which is the same data as "starts 0.004 behind the float model" and a fairer summary of it.
- **`a-pma` separably beats the certified winner on `mean_area` once both are at R=20.** Pooled: a-pma n=40, 0.827684 ± 0.001455; winner n=20, 0.825519 ± 0.000727. diff +0.002165, threshold 0.000845, **ratio 2.56 — separable**, not a tie. _An earlier version of this line predicted the opposite: that no number of refits would separate the pair, from three measurements at ratios 1.03, 1.20 and 1.22. That prediction was wrong. It generalised from a-pma's sigma RISING between R=5 and R=20 (0.00060 → 0.00145) to an assumption the winner's would rise too; it fell instead (0.00092 → 0.00073), and the winner's mean came in 0.00045 lower than its R=5 estimate. The per-severity error bars are correlated across families, since one frozen probe is applied at every point, so threshold on the mean_area sigma rather than propagating per-point errors.
- Colours are fixed per tag for the night (`runs/plots/colors.json`), so a tag keeps its colour across every figure. ## Why the eta_max 3 rows are preferred on the L1T eval file

Measured by E, 4000 events on the L1T eval file: the fraction of candidates each family actually removes. Two families deliberately fail their dead region only partially (`bench_degradation.py`: `wedge` p_drop = 0.9, `cells` p_drop ~ U(0.5, 1.0), mean 0.75; the rest are 1.0), so a family's design target for DROPPED CANDIDATES is `p_drop * s`, not `s`. So the reason to prefer the eta_max-3 rows is validity on the two families where the eta convention bites, not a better score — the area difference between the two settings is inside the probe noise (winner-l1t-eta3 vs winner-l1t: diff 0.0016, threshold 0.0013, ratio 1.27, which is a tie).

That was wrong: the error was computed against `s` rather than against `cells`' own `p_drop * s`. Corrected by E; the count-based block selection is how partial cell failure is modelled, not a defect._
